Birthplaces of Major Religious Leaders

Bethlehem, Mecca, Tehran, Nankana Sahib, Kundagrama, Lumbini and Qufu: where seven of the world’s great religious figures were born, and the countries those towns are in today.

Oct 3, 2026 6 MIN READ
By Ariyan Khan Documentary Creator & Video Editor
Rec 00:00:00:00

The people who founded or shaped the world’s great religions were born in real towns, many of which still exist. Some are famous pilgrimage cities; others are small places that most people could not find on a map. And because borders have moved so much over the centuries, several of them now lie in countries you might not expect.

I made a map of seven of these birthplaces for my Facebook page and asked a simple question: which one surprised you the most? This article goes through each of them: where the town is today, what tradition and history say about the birth, and what stands there now. It sits alongside my earlier article on where every major religion began, which looks at the faiths rather than the people.

A note on dates and places: for figures who lived many centuries ago, the sources are religious traditions written down after their lifetimes. Where historians debate a date or a location, I say so, without taking a side on matters of faith.

01 Jesus: Bethlehem

According to the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, Jesus was born in Bethlehem, a small town about ten kilometres south of Jerusalem, and grew up in Nazareth in Galilee. His birth is usually dated to around 6 to 4 BCE, under the rule of Herod the Great. Some historians think Nazareth may have been his actual birthplace, but Bethlehem is where Christian tradition has always placed it.

Today Bethlehem is in the West Bank, part of the Palestinian territories, and many people are surprised to learn that it is not in Israel. At its centre stands the Church of the Nativity, built over the cave traditionally identified as the place of birth. First built in the fourth century and rebuilt in the sixth, it is one of the oldest churches in continuous use in the world and became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012.

02 Prophet Muhammad: Mecca

The Prophet Muhammad was born in Mecca, in the west of the Arabian Peninsula, around 570 CE, a year that Islamic tradition calls the Year of the Elephant. He belonged to the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh, the tribe that dominated the city and its trade.

Mecca, in present-day Saudi Arabia, is Islam’s holiest city, home to the Kaaba and the destination of the Hajj pilgrimage. A library near the Great Mosque is traditionally said to stand on the site of the house where he was born. Entry to Mecca itself is restricted to Muslims.

Several of these towns now lie in countries where the faith of the person born there is a small minority.

03 Bahá’u’lláh: Tehran

Bahá’u’lláh, the founder of the Bahá’í Faith, was born Mírzá Ḥusayn-ʻAlí Núrí in Tehran on 12 November 1817, into a noble family from the province of Mazandaran. Tehran was then a growing capital of Qajar Persia. He later followed the Báb, was imprisoned and exiled from Iran in 1853, and spent the rest of his life in Baghdad, Istanbul, Edirne and finally Akko, in what is now northern Israel.

That is why the faith’s holiest places are in Israel rather than Iran: the Bahá’í World Centre stands on Mount Carmel in Haifa, and his shrine is at Bahjí, near Akko. His birth is celebrated by Bahá’ís around the world as one of the faith’s holy days.

04 Guru Nanak: Nankana Sahib

Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, was born in 1469 in a village called Rai Bhoi di Talwandi in the Punjab. The town is now called Nankana Sahib, in his honour, and it lies in Pakistan, about 75 kilometres west of Lahore. For many people this is the biggest surprise on the map: Sikhism’s founding sites are now mostly in Pakistan, while most Sikhs live in India.

The reason is the Partition of 1947, which divided the Punjab between the two new countries. The Gurdwara Janam Asthan, built where Guru Nanak is believed to have been born, draws pilgrims from India and around the world, especially for his birth anniversary. He spent his last years at Kartarpur, also in Pakistan, and the Kartarpur Corridor, opened in 2019, was built to let Indian pilgrims visit its gurdwara without a visa, though it has been closed at times of tension between the two countries.

05 Mahavira: Kundagrama

Mahavira, the 24th and last Tirthankara of Jainism, was born in Kundagrama, near the ancient city of Vaishali in what is now the state of Bihar in India. Jain tradition dates his birth to 599 BCE; many historians place it somewhat later. He was born a prince, renounced his life at about thirty, and spent more than twelve years as a wandering ascetic before reaching what Jains call omniscience.

The exact site of Kundagrama is debated. Many identify it with Basokund near Vaishali, while another tradition places it at Kshatriyakund in Bihar’s Jamui district, and the two main Jain sects do not fully agree. Both places have temples and receive pilgrims, especially on Mahavir Jayanti, the festival of his birth.

06 Buddha: Lumbini

Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha, was born in Lumbini, a garden near the town of Kapilavastu, probably in the fifth century BCE; the traditional date is 563 BCE, but many scholars prefer a later one. It is a common surprise that Lumbini is not in India but in southern Nepal, a few kilometres from the Indian border.

Lumbini has unusually strong evidence behind it. Around 249 BCE the Indian emperor Ashoka visited and set up a stone pillar whose inscription records that the Buddha was born there. The pillar was rediscovered in 1896 and still stands beside the Maya Devi Temple, named after the Buddha’s mother. Lumbini became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997 and is now surrounded by monasteries built by Buddhist countries from across Asia.

07 Confucius: Qufu

Confucius was born in 551 BCE in the state of Lu, in what is now Shandong province in eastern China. Tradition places his birth near Mount Ni, just outside Qufu, the state’s capital, where he later taught. He died there in 479 BCE.

Qufu has been shaped by him ever since. The Temple of Confucius, the Kong Family Mansion and the Cemetery of Confucius, where he and many of his descendants are buried, together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed in 1994. The Kong family kept detailed records of its lineage for more than two thousand years, making it one of the longest documented family trees in the world.

09 Why Some Birthplaces Surprise People

Most of the surprise comes from borders that are younger than the faiths. Lumbini was not “in Nepal” when the Buddha was born, and Nankana Sahib was not “in Pakistan” when Guru Nanak was; those countries took their present shape much later, Pakistan only in 1947. The same is true of Bethlehem, whose status has been shaped by twentieth-century wars and politics.

The other surprise is how far some faiths travelled from their birthplaces. Buddhism is now a minority in India and Nepal but the main religion of much of Southeast and East Asia, and the Bahá’í Faith, born in Tehran, has its world centre in Israel. Several of these founders also died far from where they were born, which is why a faith’s holiest sites are not always where its founder was born.
