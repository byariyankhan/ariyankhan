About the game

Puzzle: a free arrow puzzle game that trains your brain

Puzzle – Train Your Brain is a tap-away arrow puzzle you play in the browser, free, and playing on your own needs no account. Every board is the real outline of a country, filled with arrows that only slide the way they point. Tap an arrow whose path to the edge is clear and it shoots off the board; tap one that is blocked and you lose one of four hearts. Clear the board and the country appears, with a reading of how well you focused, and you move on along a 197-country world tour. If you are looking for an arrow game that rewards patience and a careful eye rather than speed, this is it.

It is built by Ariyan Khan, who makes geography, history and economy videos, and it doubles as a quiet focus exercise: a few boards a day, no feed, no timer.

How To Play

Tap, shoot, reveal

  1. Look before you tapEvery arrow slides the way it points. If the cells between its tip and the edge are empty, tapping it shoots it off the board. If another arrow sits in that path, the tap fails and you lose one of your four hearts. The blocker flashes red, and the arrow you tapped stays red: the moment its lane clears it goes by itself, no second tap needed. Not sure? Press and hold any arrow to see its lane, green if it can go, red if not — four of those a level, counted on the bar beside your hearts.
  2. Peel the board from the outside inArrows near the edge are usually free first. Clearing them opens lanes for the ones behind. There is always at least one free arrow, so if you cannot see it, press Hint: it lights one up for five seconds on the clock.
  3. Name the countryAs the board empties, the country's real outline fades in underneath. Clear the last arrow, pick its name from three options, and the level card shows the capital, population and region. Then on to the next stop of the tour.
The World Tour

197 countries, five tiers

The tour starts with shapes almost everyone knows, Italy, India, Australia, Brazil, Bangladesh, Japan, and works through the world's largest countries before finishing with Iceland. Early boards have about 90 cells and short arrows; the last tier packs 600 cells with arrows that snake up to eight cells long. Every outline is the real country from Natural Earth, drawn on an equal-area projection, so sizes are honest.

Same board for everyone. Each level has a fixed layout, so a time you share on level 12 is a fair challenge. Fail a board twice and you can skip it; you can also ask for a new layout of the same country at any time.

There is no clock to beat. Every level gives four hearts, three hints and four checks — press and hold any arrow and it tells you whether its lane is clear, green for yes and red for no, without spending a heart to find out. The difficulty follows you, not the level number: a flawless, fast clear (no heart lost, no hint) steps the boards up a tier at once, two first-try clears in a row step up too whatever hearts and hints you spent, and two losses in a row ease off a tier. Boards run from about 20 arrows on Easy to 80 and more on Master and are labelled Easy, Normal, Hard, Expert and Master. Quick consecutive taps build a combo, clearing levels back to back builds a win streak, every tenth level is a milestone, and the Today's Country board gives everyone the same bonus puzzle each day.

Why I Built This

Geography that sneaks up on you

I make videos about geography, history, culture and economies, and I animate maps for other channels. Tap-away arrow puzzles are the most addictive casual genre on the app stores, but the shapes underneath them are butterflies and trophies. Swapping those for real countries costs the player nothing, and after a few boards they can pick Mozambique out of a line-up without ever having tried to learn it.

If you prefer a slower, more deliberate way to learn the map, try my other game, Piece the World, a jigsaw of continents and countries.
