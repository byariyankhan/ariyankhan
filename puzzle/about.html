Free brain training in your browser and in the app · How to play

Free daily brain training on real paintings

Daily Training is four short games, free every day, played on public-domain paintings from The Metropolitan Museum of Art’s Open Access collection. Every round is scored out of 100, and together they make the day’s Brain Score.

  • Restore the Canvas: the painting hangs whole for a few seconds, then falls into pieces. Drag every piece back to its place.
  • The Forgery: spot the differences. Patches of a forged copy are mirrored, recoloured or taken from elsewhere in the painting; tap them.
  • Gallery Memory: paintings hang on the wall, numbered, for a moment. Hang them back in the same order.
  • The Curator’s Eye: look at one painting, then, several times, pick which of four close-ups came from it.

An arrow logic puzzle on 197 countries

Tap an arrow to send it off the board. Over 400 boards make a world tour that starts from your home country: the outlines of all 197 countries, with animal, bird, landmark and picture boards between them. Clearing a country tells you its capital, population and region, and the difficulty, from Easy to Master, follows how well you play.

Play with friends

Sign in with Google to race friends and other players online and to play in the weekly league. Playing on your own never needs an account.

Questions

Is Puzzle – Train Your Brain free?

Yes. The four Daily Training rounds are free every day, and the arrow puzzle tour is free to play. Nothing in the game can be bought: there are no in-app purchases, and gold is only a number in the game. Any advertisement is optional and rewarded: you choose to watch one, for an extra hint, heart, gold or a new puzzle.

Do I need to download anything or sign up?

No. Puzzle plays in your web browser on phones, tablets and computers, and you can add it to your home screen. Playing on your own needs no account; signing in with Google is only for Play with Friends and the weekly league.

What are the Daily Training games?

Four short rounds on real paintings: Restore the Canvas (rebuild a painting from its pieces), The Forgery (spot what is wrong in a forged copy), Gallery Memory (re-hang paintings in the order they hung) and The Curator’s Eye (pick the detail that came from the painting you saw).

How is the Brain Score worked out?

Each round is scored from 0 to 100, and the day’s Brain Score is the average of the rounds you played that day. Play at least one round a day to keep your streak going. A round gets harder after a day you score 85 or more in it, and easier after a day under 50.

How does the arrow puzzle work?

Every arrow slides the way it points. Tap an arrow and, if its path is clear, it flies off the board; if another arrow blocks it, you lose a heart (four on Easy and Normal, fewer on the harder levels). Clear every arrow to finish the board. A country board then tells you which country it was, with its capital, population and region.

Where do the paintings come from?

They are 247 public-domain works from The Metropolitan Museum of Art’s Open Access collection, and every round credits the paintings it used.
