Norway’s counties

Norway has 15 counties, or fylker, the division in use since 1 January 2024, when several of the larger counties created in 2020 were split again. Oslo, the capital, is a city and a county of its own. Nordland, which stretches far up the coast past the Arctic Circle, is long and narrow, Innlandet, the only county without a coast, is the largest, and Finnmark in the far north-east is the least crowded. The Arctic islands of Svalbard and Jan Mayen are not part of any county, so they are not on this map. The map is drawn from the earlier county borders, joined into today’s counties, so a few small border towns may sit on the old side of a line.

Covering Norway

Norway is long, about 1,700 kilometres from south to north, so most travellers combine trains, ferries, coastal ships and short flights. The Bergen Railway links Oslo to Bergen in Vestland, the gateway to the fjords, including Sognefjord and the Nærøyfjord. Geirangerfjord is in Møre og Romsdal, the Lofoten islands in Nordland, and Tromsø in Troms is a base for the northern lights. The Hurtigruten coastal ships sail all the way to Kirkenes in Finnmark.

Questions

How many counties does Norway have?

Fifteen, since 2024. From 2020 to 2023 there were 11, after a series of mergers that were partly reversed.

Which county are the fjords in?

The best-known fjords are in Vestland, around Bergen, and in Møre og Romsdal, home of Geirangerfjord.

Is Svalbard included?

No. Svalbard is outside the county system and is not on this map.
