Croatia’s counties

Croatia is divided into 20 counties, or županije, and the City of Zagreb, which has the status of a county. The country curves around Bosnia and Herzegovina: the Adriatic coast and its islands run from Istria in the north-west to Dubrovnik-Neretva in the far south, while the inland counties of Slavonia reach east to the Danube. Dubrovnik-Neretva is joined to the rest of the country by the Pelješac Bridge, opened in 2022.

Covering Croatia

Many trips follow the coast. Highlights include the hill towns of Istria, the Plitvice Lakes in Lika-Senj, Zadar and its sea organ, Krka National Park in Šibenik-Knin, Diocletian’s Palace in Split and the nearby islands of Hvar and Brač in Split-Dalmatia, and the walled city of Dubrovnik. Ferries and catamarans link the islands, and Zagreb, the capital, is the gateway to the north and east.

Questions

How many counties does Croatia have?

Twenty, plus the City of Zagreb, which has county status: 21 on this map.

Which county are the Plitvice Lakes in?

Mostly Lika-Senj, with a small part in Karlovac county.

Which county is Hvar in?

Split-Dalmatia, along with Brač, Vis and the city of Split.
