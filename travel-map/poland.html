Poland’s voivodeships

Poland is divided into 16 voivodeships, or provinces, created in their current form in 1999. Masovian, around Warsaw, is the largest and the most populous. Many are named after historical regions: Pomerania on the Baltic coast, Silesia in the south-west, and Greater and Lesser Poland, the old heartlands around Poznań and Kraków. Two voivodeships, Kuyavian-Pomeranian and Lubusz, split their government between two cities.

Covering Poland

Most trips include Warsaw and Kraków, linked by fast trains in under three hours. From Kraków, the Wieliczka salt mine and the Tatra Mountains around Zakopane are in Lesser Poland. On the coast, Gdańsk is the gateway to the Baltic, and Wrocław in Lower Silesia, Poznań and Toruń have well-kept old towns. The lakes of Masuria and the ancient Białowieża Forest in Podlaskie draw nature lovers to the north-east.

Questions

How many voivodeships does Poland have?

Sixteen, in place since a reform in 1999.

What is a voivodeship?

It is Poland’s word for a province, the largest unit of local government, led by a voivode appointed by the national government and a regional assembly.

Which voivodeship is Kraków in?

Lesser Poland (Małopolska).
