Bangladesh’s districts and divisions

Bangladesh is divided into eight divisions and 64 districts, or zila. Dhaka division has the most districts, 13, and Mymensingh, the newest division, created in 2015 from four districts of Dhaka division, and Sylhet have the fewest, four each. Rangamati, in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, is the largest district, and Narayanganj, next to the capital, is the smallest. In 2018 the government changed the English spelling of five district names to match the Bengali: Chattogram (Chittagong), Cumilla (Comilla), Barishal (Barisal), Bogura (Bogra) and Jashore (Jessore).

Covering Bangladesh

Trains, buses and river launches link the districts, and short flights reach Cox’s Bazar, Sylhet, Jashore and Saidpur from Dhaka. Popular stops include Old Dhaka and Lalbagh Fort; Sonargaon in Narayanganj; the long beach of Cox’s Bazar and St Martin’s Island; the hills of Bandarban and Sajek Valley in Rangamati; the tea gardens of Sreemangal in Moulvibazar and Jaflong and Ratargul in Sylhet; Tanguar Haor in Sunamganj; the Sundarbans mangrove forest, mostly in Satkhira, Khulna and Bagerhat, and the Sixty Dome Mosque in Bagerhat; Kuakata beach in Patuakhali; Paharpur in Naogaon; Mahasthangarh in Bogura; and Kantajew Temple in Dinajpur.

Questions

How many districts does Bangladesh have?

Sixty-four, grouped into eight divisions: Barishal, Chattogram, Dhaka, Khulna, Mymensingh, Rajshahi, Rangpur and Sylhet.

Which is the newest division?

Mymensingh, created in 2015 from the Mymensingh, Jamalpur, Netrokona and Sherpur districts of Dhaka division.

Which district is Sajek Valley in?

Rangamati, although most visitors reach it by road through Khagrachhari.

Why Chattogram and not Chittagong?

In 2018 Bangladesh changed the English spellings of five districts to follow their Bengali names: Chattogram, Cumilla, Barishal, Bogura and Jashore. The map uses the new spellings.
