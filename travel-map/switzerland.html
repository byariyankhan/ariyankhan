Switzerland’s cantons

Switzerland is a federation of 26 cantons, each with its own constitution, parliament and government. They range from Graubünden, the largest, to Basel-Stadt, which is little more than the city of Basel. Four languages are official: German is spoken in most cantons, French in the west, Italian in Ticino and part of Graubünden, and Romansh in parts of Graubünden. Bern is the federal city, where the government sits.

Covering Switzerland

Swiss trains, buses, boats and mountain railways run on one connected timetable, so many cantons are an easy day trip from each other. Popular stops include Zurich and Lucerne, the Jungfrau region in Bern, the Matterhorn above Zermatt in Valais, Lake Geneva in Geneva and Vaud, and the Italian-speaking lakes of Ticino. Panoramic routes such as the Glacier Express cross several cantons in a single day.

Questions

How many cantons does Switzerland have?

Twenty-six. Six of them, such as Basel-Stadt and Basel-Landschaft, were once counted as half-cantons.

What is the capital of Switzerland?

Bern is the federal city, where the parliament and government sit. Zurich is the largest city.

Which cantons speak French?

Geneva, Vaud, Neuchâtel and Jura are French-speaking, and Bern, Fribourg and Valais are bilingual French and German.
