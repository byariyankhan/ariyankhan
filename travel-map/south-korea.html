South Korea’s provinces and cities

South Korea has 17 top-level divisions: the special city of Seoul, six metropolitan cities (Busan, Daegu, Incheon, Gwangju, Daejeon and Ulsan), the special self-governing city of Sejong, the new administrative capital, and nine provinces. Three of the provinces, Jeju, Gangwon and North Jeolla (now called Jeonbuk State), have special self-governing status. Gyeonggi, the province around Seoul, has the most people.

Covering South Korea

South Korea is compact, and the KTX high-speed train links Seoul to Busan in under three hours, so many travellers cover several provinces in one trip. Popular stops include Seoul and the palaces of the capital, Busan’s beaches, the old royal city of Gyeongju in North Gyeongsang, the mountains of Gangwon, and the volcanic island of Jeju, reached by a short flight.

Questions

How many provinces does South Korea have?

Nine provinces, six metropolitan cities, the special city of Seoul and the special self-governing city of Sejong: 17 divisions in all.

Why is Gangwon called Gangwon State?

Gangwon became a special self-governing province, Gangwon State, in 2023, and North Jeolla became Jeonbuk State in 2024. The map uses their familiar names.

Is North Korea included?

No. This map covers South Korea; North Korea is on the Asia and world maps.
