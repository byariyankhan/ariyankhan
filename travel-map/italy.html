Italy’s 20 regions

Italy is divided into 20 regions. Five of them, Sicily, Sardinia, Trentino-South Tyrol, the Aosta Valley and Friuli-Venezia Giulia, have a special statute that gives them extra autonomy. Sicily is the largest region and Lombardy, around Milan, has the most people. Vatican City and San Marino are independent countries inside Italy, so they are not part of this map; you can mark them on the Europe map.

Covering Italy

The classic first trip links Rome in Lazio, Florence in Tuscany and Venice in the Veneto by fast train. Campania adds Naples, Pompeii and the Amalfi Coast, and the two big islands, Sicily and Sardinia, are trips of their own. Smaller regions such as Molise, Basilicata and the Aosta Valley see far fewer visitors and are often the last ones people add.

Questions

How many regions does Italy have?

Twenty, five of which have a special statute of autonomy.

Are Vatican City and San Marino included?

No. They are separate countries, so they are on the Europe and world maps instead.

Which is the largest region?

Sicily by area; Lombardy by population.

Will my map be saved?

Your marks stay in this browser. Use Copy link to keep or share your map anywhere else.
