Japan’s 47 prefectures

Japan is divided into 47 prefectures: one metropolis (Tokyo), one circuit (Hokkaido), two urban prefectures (Osaka and Kyoto) and 43 others. They are usually grouped into eight regions, from Hokkaido in the north through Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kansai, Chugoku and Shikoku to Kyushu and Okinawa in the south. Tracking the prefectures you have visited is a popular pastime in Japan itself.

Covering Japan

Most first trips follow the “golden route” from Tokyo to Kyoto and Osaka, passing through Kanagawa, Shizuoka and Aichi on the shinkansen. Hokkaido, Shikoku and Okinawa are trips of their own, and the Japan Sea coast and Tohoku see fewer foreign visitors. With fast trains and domestic flights, visiting all 47 is a goal many travellers set themselves over several trips.

Questions

How many prefectures does Japan have?

47: Tokyo Metropolis, Hokkaido, the urban prefectures of Osaka and Kyoto, and 43 other prefectures.

Which regions are they grouped into?

Usually eight: Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kansai, Chugoku, Shikoku, and Kyushu with Okinawa.

Which is the largest prefecture?

Hokkaido, by far: it covers more than a fifth of Japan’s land. Kagawa, on Shikoku, is the smallest.
