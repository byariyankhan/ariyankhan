Spain’s autonomous communities

Spain is divided into 17 autonomous communities, each with its own parliament and government, and two autonomous cities, Ceuta and Melilla, on the North African coast. The communities range from Castile and León, the largest by area, to La Rioja and the Balearic Islands; Andalusia has the most people. The Canary Islands lie off the coast of Africa, so on this map they are moved closer and shown in a box.

Covering Spain

Madrid, Catalonia and Andalusia are where most trips begin, and fast AVE trains link many regional capitals. The green north, from Galicia through Asturias and Cantabria to the Basque Country, feels very different from the dry interior of Castile, and the two island groups, the Balearics in the Mediterranean and the Canaries in the Atlantic, are trips of their own. For why so much of the interior is quiet, see my article on why so much of Spain is empty.

Questions

How many regions does Spain have?

Seventeen autonomous communities and two autonomous cities, Ceuta and Melilla.

Are Ceuta and Melilla included?

Yes, you can mark them. They are counted on their own line, because the 17 are the autonomous communities.

Why are the Canary Islands in a box?

They lie far to the south-west, off Africa, so like Alaska and Hawaii on a US map they are moved closer and framed.

Will my map be saved?

Your marks stay in this browser. Use Copy link to keep or share your map anywhere else.
