What counts as visiting a state?

There is no official rule, so most people choose their own. A common one is that you count a state if you spent time there, such as staying the night or exploring, and not if you only changed planes at its airport or drove straight through. Some travellers are stricter and only count states where they slept, ate a meal or saw a sight. Whatever rule you choose, use it for every state so your count means the same thing from start to finish.

Visiting all 50 states

Seeing every state is a popular travel goal. Alaska and Hawaii usually take the most planning, because they are far from the other 48 and are best reached by plane. Among the contiguous states, the ones away from major routes, such as North Dakota, are often said to be the last on people’s lists. A good way to fill the map is to group neighbouring states into one road trip: New England fits into a week, and the Four Corners region links Arizona, Utah, Colorado and New Mexico.

Questions

Is the states visited map free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark beyond the small site address on the image, and no limit.

Does it include Alaska and Hawaii?

Yes. Both are shown in the usual inset positions below the other 48 states, and both count toward your 50.

Is Washington, D.C. included?

No. The count is the 50 states, so D.C. and the US territories are not on this map. You can mark Puerto Rico, Guam and the other territories on the world map.

Will my map be saved?

Your marks are kept in this browser, so they are still there when you come back on the same device. To keep them anywhere else, use Copy link: the link holds your whole map.
