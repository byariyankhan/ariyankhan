How many national parks are there?

There are 63 national parks in the United States. They are only part of the National Park System, which has more than 400 sites, including national monuments, historic sites, seashores and battlefields. The title “national park” is given by Congress, usually to the largest and most varied of these places. The oldest is Yellowstone, established in 1872 as the first national park in the world; the newest is New River Gorge, in 2020.

Which states have the most national parks?

California has the most, with 9, followed by Alaska with 8 and Utah with 5, whose five parks are often called the “Mighty 5”. Some parks cross state lines: Yellowstone reaches into Montana and Idaho, Great Smoky Mountains straddles North Carolina and Tennessee, and Death Valley extends into Nevada. 30 states have at least one national park; the other 20 have none.

Planning to visit them all

Seeing all 63 is a long-term goal for many travellers. The parks in Alaska, American Samoa and the Virgin Islands take the most planning, and some, such as Gates of the Arctic and Kobuk Valley, have no roads at all. In the lower 48, parks cluster in a few regions, so they can be combined into road trips: Utah’s five parks fit into a week or two, as do California’s Yosemite, Sequoia and Kings Canyon. An annual America the Beautiful pass covers entrance fees at every national park; check the National Park Service website for current prices, opening times and any reservation systems before you go.

Questions

How many national parks are in the US?

Sixty-three. The National Park System has more than 400 sites in total, but only 63 carry the title of national park.

Which is the most visited national park?

Great Smoky Mountains, on the border of North Carolina and Tennessee, is the most visited national park.

Which is the largest national park?

Wrangell–St. Elias in Alaska, at more than 13 million acres, larger than several US states.

Are American Samoa and the Virgin Islands included?

Yes. Both are national parks and count toward the 63; they are shown in two small boxes off the East Coast.
