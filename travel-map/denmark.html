Denmark’s regions

Denmark has been divided into five regions since 2007. The Capital Region covers Copenhagen, northern Zealand and the island of Bornholm in the Baltic. Zealand covers the rest of the island of Zealand and the islands to the south, Southern Denmark covers southern Jutland and Funen, and Central and North Denmark cover the rest of Jutland. Greenland and the Faroe Islands are self-governing parts of the Kingdom of Denmark and are not on this map.

Covering Denmark

Bridges and trains connect most of the country. Many trips start in Copenhagen and add Kronborg Castle at Helsingør in the Capital Region, Roskilde and its Viking Ship Museum in Zealand, Odense and the original Legoland at Billund in Southern Denmark, Aarhus in Central Denmark, and Skagen at the northern tip of Jutland in North Denmark, where two seas meet. Bornholm is reached by ferry or a short flight.

Questions

How many regions does Denmark have?

Five, created in 2007 to replace the old counties.

Which region is Legoland in?

Southern Denmark: the original Legoland is in Billund.

Are Greenland and the Faroe Islands included?

No. They are self-governing parts of the Kingdom of Denmark, outside the five regions.
