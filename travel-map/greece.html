Greece’s regions

Greece is divided into 13 administrative regions. Attica, around Athens, has more than a third of the country’s people. Three regions are made up of islands: the Ionian Islands off the west coast, the North Aegean, and the South Aegean, which includes the Cyclades, such as Santorini and Mykonos, and the Dodecanese, such as Rhodes. Crete is a region of its own. Mount Athos, a self-governing community of monasteries, can also be marked and is counted on its own line.

Covering Greece

Many first trips combine Athens with a few islands by ferry. On the mainland, Delphi is in Central Greece, the monasteries of Meteora in Thessaly, Olympia and Patras in Western Greece, and Nafplio and ancient Mycenae in the Peloponnese. Thessaloniki is the hub of the north. The islands are spread across five regions, so island hopping is one of the quickest ways to add to your count.

Questions

How many regions does Greece have?

Thirteen administrative regions, plus the self-governing monastic state of Mount Athos.

Which region is Santorini in?

The South Aegean, along with the other Cyclades and the Dodecanese islands.

Can I mark Mount Athos?

Yes. It is counted on its own line, outside the 13 regions. Entry is limited to men with a special permit.
