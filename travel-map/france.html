The regions of France

France has 18 regions: 13 in Europe, including Corsica, and five overseas, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion and Mayotte. The 13 European regions took their present shape in 2016, when 22 older regions were merged. This map shows those 13; the overseas regions are far away on other continents, and the countries around them are on the world map. Nouvelle-Aquitaine is the largest region and Île-de-France, around Paris, has the most people.

Covering France

Paris and Île-de-France are where most visitors start, and the TGV network reaches most regions in a few hours. Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur has the Riviera and the lavender of Provence, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes has Lyon and the Alps, and Normandy and Brittany face the Channel and the Atlantic. Corsica, in the Mediterranean, is reached by ferry or plane. For more on what shaped the country, see my article on why France’s geography is so powerful.

Questions

How many regions does France have?

Eighteen: thirteen in Europe, including Corsica, and five overseas regions.

Why aren’t the overseas regions on the map?

They are thousands of kilometres away, in the Caribbean, South America and the Indian Ocean, so this map covers the 13 regions of mainland France and Corsica.

Which is the largest region?

Nouvelle-Aquitaine by area; Île-de-France by population.

Will my map be saved?

Your marks stay in this browser. Use Copy link to keep or share your map anywhere else.
