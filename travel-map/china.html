China’s provinces and regions

Mainland China is divided into 31 province-level divisions: 22 provinces, five autonomous regions, such as Xinjiang and Tibet (Xizang), and four municipalities run directly by the central government: Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin and Chongqing. Hong Kong and Macau, the two special administrative regions, can also be marked and are counted on their own line. Xinjiang is the largest division by area and Guangdong has the most people. Taiwan is not part of this map; it can be marked on the Asia and world maps.

Covering China

China’s high-speed rail network, the largest in the world, makes it possible to see several provinces in one trip. A classic first route links Beijing, Xi’an in Shaanxi, home of the Terracotta Army, and Shanghai. Sichuan has Chengdu and its pandas, Guangxi the karst hills around Guilin, and Yunnan its mountains and old towns. The western regions, Tibet, Xinjiang and Qinghai, are vast and take the most planning; visitors to Tibet need a special permit, so check the current rules before travelling.

Questions

How many provinces does China have?

Mainland China has 31 province-level divisions: 22 provinces, five autonomous regions and four municipalities. Hong Kong and Macau are special administrative regions.

Are Hong Kong and Macau included?

Yes, you can mark them. They are counted on their own line, outside the 31.

Which is the largest province?

Xinjiang is the largest division by area; Guangdong has the most people.

Will my map be saved?

Your marks stay in this browser. Use Copy link to keep or share your map anywhere else.
