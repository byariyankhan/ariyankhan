Colombia’s departments

Colombia is divided into 32 departments and the Capital District of Bogotá, which is separate from Cundinamarca, the department around it. The country spans Caribbean and Pacific coasts, three ranges of the Andes, the plains of the Orinoco and the Amazon rainforest. The islands of San Andrés and Providencia, more than 700 kilometres away in the Caribbean, are a department of their own; on this map they are moved closer and shown in a box.

Covering Colombia

Distances are long and the mountains slow the roads, so most travellers fly between regions. Popular stops include Bogotá, Medellín in Antioquia, the walled city of Cartagena in Bolívar, Tayrona National Park near Santa Marta in Magdalena, the coffee region of Caldas, Quindío and Risaralda, and Leticia in Amazonas, on the Amazon river. Before you travel, check your government’s current advice for each region.

Questions

How many departments does Colombia have?

Thirty-two, plus the Capital District of Bogotá: 33 on this map.

Is Bogotá part of Cundinamarca?

No. Bogotá is a capital district of its own, although it is also the seat of Cundinamarca’s government.

Where is the coffee region?

Mostly in Caldas, Quindío and Risaralda, with parts of Antioquia, Tolima and Valle del Cauca.
