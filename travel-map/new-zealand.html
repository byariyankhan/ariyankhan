New Zealand’s regions

New Zealand is divided into 16 regions: nine on the North Island and seven on the South Island, with Stewart Island part of Southland. Auckland has about a third of the country’s people, and Canterbury is the largest region by area. Wellington, the capital, sits at the southern tip of the North Island. The Chatham Islands, far out in the Pacific, have their own council and are not drawn on this map.

Covering New Zealand

Most visitors drive, and a classic trip covers both islands. On the North Island: the Bay of Islands in Northland, Auckland, the Hobbiton set and Waitomo caves in Waikato, and the geothermal parks of Rotorua in Bay of Plenty. A ferry crosses Cook Strait from Wellington to Marlborough. On the South Island: Abel Tasman in Tasman, the glaciers of the West Coast, Aoraki / Mount Cook in Canterbury, Queenstown in Otago, and Milford Sound in Southland.

Questions

How many regions does New Zealand have?

Sixteen: nine on the North Island and seven on the South Island.

Which region is Queenstown in?

Otago, on the South Island.

Are the Chatham Islands included?

No. They are a separate territory far to the east, outside the 16 regions.
