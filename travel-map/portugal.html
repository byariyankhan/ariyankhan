Portugal’s districts and autonomous regions

Mainland Portugal is divided into 18 districts, each named after its capital, from Viana do Castelo in the north to Faro, which covers the Algarve, in the south. The two island groups in the Atlantic, the Azores and Madeira, are autonomous regions with their own governments. The islands are far out at sea, so on this map they are moved closer and shown in boxes, the Azores at half size.

Covering Portugal

Most trips start in Lisbon or Porto, which are linked by fast trains in about three hours. From Lisbon, Sintra and the coast are day trips; from Porto, the Douro valley’s vineyards run inland through Vila Real and Viseu. The Algarve, in Faro district, is the beach coast, and the Alentejo, in Évora, Beja and Portalegre, is quiet, rolling countryside. The Azores and Madeira are reached by short flights from the mainland.

Questions

How many districts does Portugal have?

Eighteen on the mainland, plus the two autonomous regions of the Azores and Madeira: 20 on this map.

Why are the Azores and Madeira in boxes?

They lie far out in the Atlantic, so like Alaska and Hawaii on a US map they are moved closer; the Azores are drawn at half size so they fit.

Where is the Algarve?

The Algarve is the Faro district, along Portugal’s south coast.
