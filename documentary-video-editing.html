What I Do

You bring footage. I build the story.

Send your raw material, notes, and references. I'll shape it into a structured, polished documentary edit ready for your audience.

Documentary video editing starts with finding the clearest story inside the raw footage. I organize interviews, voiceover, b-roll, and research into a narrative flow that feels focused and easy to follow.

A strong documentary edit needs rhythm, silence, and timing in the right places. I shape scenes with natural cuts, purposeful pauses, and transitions that keep viewers engaged without making the story feel rushed.

B-roll and archive material make documentary storytelling more visual. I place stock footage, screen material, photos, and supporting clips where they add context, clarity, and emotional weight.

I balance mixed footage so interviews, b-roll, and archival clips feel like one polished film. A clean documentary color grade improves visual consistency and gives the final video a more cinematic tone.

Professional documentary editing depends on clear dialogue and balanced sound. I clean interviews, level narration, reduce noise, and mix music so the story feels immersive without overpowering the message.

Titles, lower thirds, captions, maps, and callouts help viewers understand names, places, dates, and context. I keep graphics clean and documentary-friendly so they support the story instead of distracting from it.

Problems I Solve

Sound Familiar?

These are the most common struggles documentary filmmakers and brands face before working with a professional editor. I fix all of them.

Hours of raw interviews with no clear story structure or narrative arc

Documentary video editing is not just cutting clips together. I organize interviews, voiceover, b-roll, and research into a clear narrative that keeps viewers following the story.

Flat footage that feels like a recording, not a cinematic documentary

A strong documentary edit needs pacing, tension, pauses, and visual contrast. I shape scenes so raw footage feels more cinematic without making the story feel fake.

Poor audio mix — inconsistent levels between interviews, narration, and music

Viewers forgive imperfect footage faster than bad sound. I clean dialogue, balance narration, reduce noise, and mix music so the documentary feels professional.

Missing b-roll and cutaways that make interviews boring to watch

B-roll, archive footage, photos, maps, and visual cutaways give context to interview-heavy documentaries. I place them where they explain the story and keep the edit moving.

Color grade that looks amateur — no cinematic tone or visual consistency

Mixed cameras, lighting, and archival clips can make a documentary feel uneven. I balance color and contrast so the final edit feels cohesive and intentional.

Missed deadlines because the edit took weeks longer than expected

Documentary projects can stall when footage review and story assembly take too long. I keep the editing workflow organized so the project moves toward delivery.
