How To Play

Drag, drop, beat the clock

  1. Pick a board and a difficultyStart with World (seven continents) on Easy if you have never seen an outline map, or go straight to Africa on Hard if you are a geography channel regular.
  2. Drag pieces from the tray onto the boardThe piece appears at its true size while you drag. Drop it close to where it belongs and it snaps in with a fact about that country. On a phone you can also tap a piece, then tap the board.
  3. Finish fast, finish cleanThe timer starts on your first drag. Wrong drops and hints cost accuracy, accuracy decides your stars, and your best time per board is saved. Share it and see if a friend can beat it.
The Boards

Seven levels, 161 pieces

Every board is a real map. The shapes come from Natural Earth, the public-domain dataset used by newsrooms and atlases, drawn on an equal-area projection so Brazil, Algeria and Kazakhstan are exactly as big as they should be next to their neighbours.

Countries on two continents go where their capital is. Russia is a Europe piece, Turkey, Kazakhstan, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Armenia are Asia pieces, Egypt is Africa and Panama is North America. Borders follow Natural Earth's de facto policy, showing who controls an area today. Micro-states and tiny islands are placed for you so the game stays playable on a phone.

FAQ

Questions before you play

Yes. There is no account, no download and no paywall. The game runs in your browser, your best times are saved on your own device, and nothing is sent to a server.

Easy shows every country's outline and name on the board. Normal keeps the outlines but only names the pieces in your tray. Hard shows the continent as one blank silhouette and hides every name, so you place countries purely by shape and memory.

Accuracy is the number of pieces divided by pieces plus wrong drops, with each hint counting as two wrong drops. 90% or better earns three stars, 70% earns two, anything else one. The timer starts on your first drag and stops when the last piece snaps in.

Countries that span two continents are placed by their capital. Russia (Moscow) is in the Europe level, Turkey (Ankara), Kazakhstan, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Armenia and Cyprus are in Asia, Egypt (Cairo) is in Africa, and Panama is in North America. Indonesia sits in Asia. The World level uses whole-continent shapes.

Micro-states and small islands such as Vatican City, Monaco, Singapore, Bahrain, the Gambia or the Caribbean islands would be a few pixels wide on a phone screen, so the game places them for you and shows them dimmed. Every other country is a piece you drag yourself.

Each level uses an equal-area projection, so the pieces have the same relative size as the real countries. Mercator, the projection most web maps use, inflates anything near the poles, which is why Greenland usually looks as big as Africa when it is actually fourteen times smaller.
