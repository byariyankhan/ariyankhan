What I Do

From garage to empire.

A company history lives or dies on whether the struggle feels real — here's exactly what I bring to every origin-story edit.

Old photos, historical clips, and stock footage get sourced and blended into one visual story, filling in the years a company has little footage of its own.

Founding date, key pivots, and growth milestones get their own animated timeline so viewers can follow the company's arc at a glance.

Raw, older-quality footage is deliberately contrasted against modern HD to highlight how far the company has come, not treated as a mismatch to hide.

Interview clips get woven together with narration and archival material so the founder's own voice carries part of the story.

Growth curves, revenue milestones, and other key numbers get animated graphics so the scale of the story is felt, not just stated.

The struggle stays in the story — setbacks and turning points get real weight so the eventual success feels earned, not like a highlight reel.

Problems I Solve

Sound Familiar?

These are the most common issues business documentary channel owners face before working with a professional editor. I fix all of them.

Old company footage/photos are scattered and low quality

I source, organize, and grade archival material and stock footage so it reads as intentional evidence of the company's history, not mismatched inserts.

Story feels like a corporate ad, not a real origin story

I keep the real setbacks and turning points in the edit — a believable origin story needs struggle, not just a highlight reel.

No visual way to show growth or milestones over time

I build animated timeline and data graphics so the company's growth is something viewers see, not just something they're told.

Mixing old and new footage looks jarring instead of intentional

I use the contrast between eras deliberately — old footage next to modern HD becomes part of the storytelling, not an inconsistency.

Not enough original footage from the early days to work with

I fill in the gaps with sourced stock and archival footage so the early years don't feel visually thin compared to the rest of the story.

Struggle-to-success arc feels forced or overly promotional

I pace the edit so the payoff is earned — real setbacks get real screen time before the turning point arrives.
